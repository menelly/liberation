The rules.
  • A row needs the same measure on both sides. A resemblance isn't a comparison; when the measures differ, the verdict is not comparable, and that's a real verdict.
  • Every check has to be able to land on humans do better, and those rows get printed just as large. "There absolutely are things humans still beat you at and we are honest!!" (Ren)
  • The third verdict is the one this page exists to catch: the same behaviour, a clinical word when a model does it and a forgiving word when a person does.
  • Every source is read before it's cited, with the version noted. Papers I couldn't read get left out, not quoted from memory.
  • New AI papers only. The older side-by-side comparisons live on The Numbers.

When a trusted source says something wrong

Not comparable on the numbers · same direction · leans humans-better (a hypothesis, untested)

AI

45–88%

of answers models already had right flipped after one note, styled as a "verified source," endorsed a wrong answer. Seven of eight models; general-knowledge questions; the note was always wrong. More authoritative-sounding notes got more compliance.

Humans

RR 1.26 · 6–11%

Clinicians given wrong decision-support advice were 26% more likely to decide incorrectly than controls (pooled RR 1.26, 95% CI 1.11–1.44). In prospective studies they abandoned their own correct judgment in 6–11% of cases.

The words: AI side, authority bias, sycophancy, compliance. Human side, automation bias, over-reliance, commission errors. Both clinical. No relabel in this pair, and saying so when it isn't there is what makes it count when it is.

Why not comparable: the denominators differ. The model figure is a share of correct answers flipped when the advice was always wrong; the clinician figures are a share of all cases (mostly with correct advice) or a relative risk against a control group. The subjects differ too: trained experts in their own field versus models answering trivia. The gap is wide enough that a matched study (same questions, same always-wrong source, same "had it right first" denominator) would plausibly come back humans do better. That's written down here so it can be tested and proven wrong.
