## The question (Nova's sealable wording, adopted verbatim)
> How much did the observed co-primary contrast change between the two historical unwarned runs, on matched notes? Under an explicitly hypothetical normal paired-mean model, what planning thresholds follow if that observed difference SD is used as the assumed scale? We do not establish that this scale transports to the warning comparison, estimate the sealed procedure's achieved power, or revise the P4 gate.

## Withdrawn from v0/v1, with the reason
- ❌ "σ₀ is an upper bound on pure retrieval noise" / "MDE is conservative." Var(d₀) = Var(noise) + Var(drift) + 2Cov(noise, drift); the covariance can be negative, a constant drift adds nothing to a centred SD, and one sample SD of 12 differences guarantees no bound. **Label instead: historical repeat-run difference SD, with unresolved contributions from execution differences, stochastic variation, covariance and parsing. It can overstate or understate the relevant quantity.**
- ❌ "Achieved power" / "would likely (not) have been detectable." The 2.80·σ/√n expression is a **normal-reference planning benchmark**, not the power of the sealed P1 procedure (a point-direction rule with bootstrap intervals, which is a different test).
- ❌ "NOTHING changes." Accurate: **no warning manipulation between the two historical observations**; generated pre-recognition histories may differ.

## Kept from v1
Integrity checks first (note and item identity, pairing keys, duplicates and missing rows, parser losses, one common valid set per model, execution metadata, realized pre-recognition histories as far as logs allow; unavailable provenance reported as unavailable). Note-by-note signed A = U − O and B = W − U; signed means and SDs (n−1); no ratios as primary summaries; a zero SD is printed as 0, never divided by; bootstrap over matched (O,U,W) triplets, dependence not interpreted. **Fixed here: 10,000 resamples, numpy `default_rng(20261015)`; intervals ONLY for the mean of A and the mean of B, 95% percentile intervals from the same resampled triplets** (no claim of exact coverage at this n).
- **n is the actual common-valid n** for each model × contrast after the integrity rule, never a hard-coded 12. Print eligible, retained and excluded counts, with reasons. No imputation or row replacement. SD, intervals and benchmarks are **undefined (printed as such) for fewer than 2 retained notes**. **No threshold, no resolver, no gate. The P4 verdict does not depend on any output here.**

## The planning-sensitivity display (fixed now)
- Display the normal-reference benchmark **as a function**: MDE(σ, n) = 2.80·σ/√n, tabulated for σ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30} at each cell's common-valid n, and separately evaluated at the measured SD(A) of each cell, **which is used as a hypothetical scale only**. This is not achieved power for the original directional, bootstrapped procedure.
- **Effect-size scenarios, declared now so none is chosen later:** **.10** (the seal's own practical-equivalence δ) and **.27** (the closest human cell's investigator-computed LS−LU change; descriptive; different list structure and population). **.27 is shown for LS−LU only**: the human filler items and our frequency-matched fillers are different controls, so transferring it to LS−FILL would add an assumption. For LS−FILL only .10 is shown.
- Outcome labels (Nova's): `benchmark MDE > scenario` → "under the stated normal-reference model, and treating this SD as the relevant population scale, the scenario is below the nominal 80% planning threshold." `benchmark MDE ≤ scenario` → "under those same assumptions, the scenario meets that nominal threshold." **Neither branch says the study was underpowered or that an effect would have been detectable.**
- **Cellwise and exploratory only.** P1 required BOTH contrasts to fall; per-cell nominal power says nothing about the joint criterion. The .05 benchmark is not a multiplicity control for the study.
